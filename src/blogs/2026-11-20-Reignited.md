I *loved* Tengen Toppa Gurren Lagann.

![Gurren Lagann Poster, a bunch of anime BS that will fill you with the fighting spirit!](/assets/shortform/blog/2026-11-20-Reignited/gurrennlagann.jpg)

I've started watching Gurren Lagann before and always fell off by episode 12. This has never been the series' fault, I can just be very flighty when it comes to finishing shows. I knew I liked it. I like GAINAX the studio that made it. I like Neon Genesis Evangelion which is the polar opposite of this in terms of structure, pacing, tone but by the same studio and these two properties are inherently linked. I love mecha animes! I'm a huge Gundam nerd! But I never finished this.

Well that changed! I wrapped up watching this earlier this week and it left me thinking about something. Spoilers ahead!

A key plot point of the anime is that humanity has been forced underground and humans on the surface are hunted down by Beastmen (exactly what it sounds like, examples at bottom of post) in Gunmen mechs. Our collective culture has been replaced - nothing really exists of the world before we were driven underground. This is important for what it has me thinking about. Another key point is at some point in the anime people return to the surface then there is a seven year time jump. In those seven years off screen the following things are reintroduced to society:

Cars. Traffic jams. Suburban tract housing. Urban sprawl. High heels and neckties. The Japanese cultural love of overly bureaucratic institutions. Taxes! 

When you are given a blank slate to work with I find it very interesting what assumptions people bring with them. Now I'm not saying the people writing this anime thought about these choices and it was intentional, and I'm not saying it wasn't intentional. This could be part of the satire.

A detailed breakdown of my issues here:

Cars/Traffic jams: In a world with mechs that can fly without using fuel I have a hard time believing we'd avoid mass transit like we have in the US and I can't believe truck traffic wouldn't be flying. Like individual cars (that are shown to have LEGS sometimes not just tires) are the HARDER solution here.

Urban Sprawl/Suburban tract houses: look the suburbs are a pox upon humanity. They destroy your sense of community, they're expensive and impossible for cities to maintain without constantly growing, they're bad for the environment on multiple levels, and aesthetically they're just bogus. I cannot believe that given a blank slate the white picket fence neighborhood is the pinnacle of human desire.

High Heels and Neckties: So to buy into this, I have to accept a universe in which Louis the XIVth, the Sun King - some might say the Fun King - is remembered when the rest of our history is forgotten. I have a big problem with that in particular, but it's exacerbated by the fact that I, sitting here writing this, would be happy going back in time and [kicking a young Louis in the nuts as he admired his Croatian mercenaries garb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necktie#Origins). He's less responsible for high heels, those have a longer history but he was a big proponent of them so he gets the blame for that too. I just can't accept this.

The Japanese style of extremely bureaucracy heavy government is probably the least objectionable of these, that could have survived the fall of humanity on inertia alone. They don't really show anything like that in the world before humans returning to the surface, and we do see some of what underground life looks like, and it's more like medieval villages. So again - for me - this is a pass dawg.

Taxes: it's a world in which fighting spirit can literally break the laws of physics. It's a world of plenty. It's a world of magical technology. It's probably if not post scarcity, very close to post scarcity and I have a hard time imagining people existing at a continuous near riot level over taxes in that world.

Why bring these up?

I think the assumptions we bring into fictional worlds are interesting and I think we can create a more interesting world when we examine those in detail and are willing to kill them off if they don't stand up to scrutiny. That being said the anime is awesome and if you're one of the five people into this kind of thing that haven't seen it you should watch it.
